Who is in control, and who is in a coin flip?

Key Takeaways

  • 3 divisions have a clear favorite: 49ers (12.3 expected wins), Chiefs (12.2 expected wins), Jaguars (11.5 expected wins), each at least 1.5 wins clear of second.
  • 5 are open: Falcons lead the NFC South by 0.2, Eagles lead the NFC East by 1.1, Ravens lead the AFC North by 1.1, Vikings lead the NFC North by 1.3, Bills lead the AFC East by 1.4.
  • The Broncos project to 10.2 wins, second in the AFC West, with the 29th-hardest remaining schedule in the league.

The whole league sits within 3.5 points from hardest to easiest, less than a strong team's home-field edge, so schedule shifts a projection by a game or so, not a tier. Chargers carry the hardest road; Bengals pair the softest slate with the top rating, which is why they lead the projection. The Broncos are 4th-easiest, part of why they project near .500 despite the Week 1 hit.

How the projection works

For each remaining game the projected home margin is Todd's formula: home-field advantage plus the difference in ratings, with a flat 0.2 at the eight neutral-site games. That margin becomes a win probability by assuming results scatter around the projection with a spread of 13.5 points, which is my parameter and not part of his sheet. A team favored by 3 wins about 59% of the time; favored by 10, about 77%. Expected wins add those probabilities to the wins already in hand. The favorite column is what the sheet would say if it picked every game outright, and the gap between the two columns shows how many coin flips a team faces.

The update factor behind these ratings is one tenth of each game's surprise, softened from one fifth at ΖΓ636's suggestion so no single week dominates the season view.

Ratings are frozen at this week's values. A bad week counts for the whole season until the next update moves it, so treat the table as a snapshot that will be rerun each week.
