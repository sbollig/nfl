The readCincinnati's case is Joe Burrow. He leads the league's 32 leading passers in completion rate (74.7%), touchdowns (27) and yards a game (310.6), and he has thrown to a five-game winning streak in which the Bengals scored 31 or more every week. Washington's defense has given up 223 points in nine games. The Commanders' case is the run game and a quarterback who protects the ball: they run for 4.3 a carry, and Jayden Daniels has two interceptions in 213 attempts. Mack makes Cincinnati the winner at 64% but makes the line Commanders +4.7, so the 6.5 points are the value. He also leans Under 52.5, because the market has already moved that total up to 54.5.

Reassessment Mack says Bengals but takes the points, and leans Under

Mack versus the market. Mack has Cincinnati winning 64% of the time; ESPN's Matchup Predictor says 76.2%. Mack's line is Commanders +4.7, while the market's frozen number is +6.5, so the pick is Commanders +6.5. On the total, Mack is Under 52.5. The ESPN page now shows 54.5, which puts the market further from Mack. The spread opened at Bengals −1.5, so the market has moved five points toward Cincinnati.

What would change it. If Risner and Hinton both sit and Patrick clears protocol, the line edge shrinks. If Croskey-Merritt is activated, Washington's run-the-clock case gets stronger, and so does the Under. If Okonkwo's back keeps him out, Washington loses its third-leading receiver. A Dugger absence matters only for depth behind Battle and Cook.

Mack's view (judgment). Cincinnati should win. But Washington runs for 4.3 a carry, rarely turns it over, and is 2-2-1 at home with every loss inside 17 points. Seven points of cushion is a lot to give on the road.
