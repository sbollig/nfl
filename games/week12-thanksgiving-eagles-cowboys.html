Where is Joe Milton? his two starts against the other 31 teams' leading passers, through Week 11, with Prescott alongside

Answer: last or next to last in almost everything, on a two-game sample, and facing the team that cut him loose. Put Milton's line in for Philadelphia against the other 31 teams' leading passers and he is 32nd in completion rate, yards per game, touchdown passes and sack rate, 31st in passer rating, and tied for last in yards per attempt. The one thing he has done is protect the ball in the air: no interceptions in 55 attempts, though he has lost a fumble in each start. Prescott on the same list: 2nd in touchdown passes (25), 3rd in yards per game (277.2), 9th in rating (101.0), and second-best at avoiding sacks. In his last three games he has 1,003 yards, 11 touchdowns and one interception (ESPN, Todd Archer, Nov 22).

Coaching staffs tenure, experience, and what the beat has said this season

Eagles

  • Nick Sirianni, head coach since Jan 2021; 62–33 in the regular season and 6–4 in the playoffs through 2025, with a Super Bowl LIX win and a Super Bowl LVII loss, and 3–7 this year. On Milton: "He works extremely hard with everything, really love his approach, like his personality." (ESPN, Tim McManus, Nov 19)
  • Sean Mannion, offensive coordinator and play-caller, 34, in his first year as a coordinator. Sirianni kept him on the calls after the bye; through nine games the offense led the league in three-and-out rate at 35.7% (ESPN, Nov 19). T.J. Paganetti was added as a consultant during the bye.
  • Vic Fangio, defensive coordinator. His defense has 10 sacks in the last two games and blitzed Rodgers twice on 53 dropbacks.
  • Michael Clay, special teams coordinator; Parks Frazier coaches the quarterbacks; Josh Grizzard is offensive passing game coordinator.

Cowboys

  • Brian Schottenheimer, head coach since Jan 24, 2025; 7–9–1 last year, 5–6 this year. On stringing wins together: "When the clock says zero, we have to have more points than the other team." (ESPN, Todd Archer, Nov 24). Dallas has not won consecutive games in a year.
  • Klayton Adams, offensive coordinator. The offense has 91 points in its last two games, the third most in a two-game span in team history (ESPN, Nov 24).
  • Christian Parker, defensive coordinator, 34, stripped of play-calling before the Tennessee game but still running meetings: "We haven't tackled like we should." (ESPN, Nov 19)
  • Mike Pettine, senior defensive assistant, now calls the defense from the sideline. He was the Browns' head coach in 2014–15 and last called plays as Green Bay's coordinator in 2020; his first game was five sacks and 30 points allowed.
  • Nick Sorensen, special teams coordinator; Steve Shimko coaches the quarterbacks.

Missed tackles both teams' season totals, and who paid for it Sunday

Philadelphia: 54 missed tackles, tied for 7th-most in the NFL; Dallas: 50, tied for 15th (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, published Nov 18; the article does not say which week it runs through). Cleveland has the most at 79 and Detroit the fewest at 25.

It cost an Eagle his job on Sunday. Riq Woolen was pulled late in the first half against Pittsburgh after a couple of missed tackles and sat until Quinyon Mitchell's injury put him back in. Woolen: "The DB position is a mental position more than physicality." Sirianni said the team "wanted to reset there a little bit" and that "we're going to need him moving forward" (ESPN, Tim McManus, Nov 22). With Jonathan Jones not practicing, Woolen is likely back in the lineup against Lamb and Pickens.

Dallas's problem shows up in yards, not the count. The Cowboys are mid-table in missed tackles but allow 5.2 yards a carry and have given up 13 runs of 20-plus. Their own coordinator said they have tackled well only "in waves" (ESPN, Nov 19). On Sunday Carnell Tate caught a ball on the ground, got up untouched and ran 59 yards for a touchdown (AP, Nov 22).

Why it matters Thursday. Barkley's 38-yard touchdown Sunday and his 62 yards on seven carries in October are the Eagles' route to points. Javonte Williams, with 155 yards in the first meeting, is Dallas's route to finishing the game.

Reassessment Mack's pick against the market, and what Wednesday's report could move

Where the board stands (Tuesday). Mack's pick and the market are both frozen at the board build, and they agree on the side.

  • Line: Mack Cowboys −9.5 · market Cowboys −8.5 (DraftKings via ESPN)
  • Cowboys win probability: Mack 76% · ESPN Matchup Predictor 82.9% · ESPN FPI Cowboys by 9.4
  • Straight-up pick: Cowboys · market moneyline Cowboys −471, Eagles +364
  • Spread side: Cowboys −8.5, with one point of room between Mack's number and the market's
  • Total: Under 47.5 · the total opened at 49.5 (ESPN betting story, Nov 24)

How they compare. A one-point gap is agreement, not a strong lean. Mack's 76% is lower than ESPN's 82.9%, so the ratings are a little less sure of Dallas straight up than ESPN's model even while making the spread a point bigger. The first meeting was a 17-point Dallas win on the road, against McKee rather than Milton.

What would change it. For Philadelphia: Mitchell's status on Wednesday's final report is the biggest input, since without him Woolen and Ringo are the outside corners against Lamb and Pickens. For Dallas: Booker and Ferguson have not practiced and are planned out here; Winters and Barham moving from limited to available would help a run defense that needs it. Prescott's left hand was a full participant both days and is not a concern on the report.

Mack's view (judgment, not the ratings). The side is comfortable. The Eagles have not scored more than 24 points in a game all season and Milton has yet to throw a touchdown pass. The Under is the part I trust less: Dallas's last four games produced 63, 50, 86 and 81 points, and the first meeting had 59. The Under needs the Eagles' pass rush to keep showing up and the Eagles' offense to keep being what it has been, which had 30 and 35 total points in its last two games.
