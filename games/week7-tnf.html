The readTwo 4-2 teams, and the line says Chicago by a field goal. Mack's board agrees almost exactly (Bears −2.8 against a market −3), so there is no spread edge here; the pick is the Bears to win and the Patriots to cover. The game turns on New England's interior line: both starting guards, Alijah Vera-Tucker and Ben Brown, are listed doubtful (ESPN, Oct 21) and Vrabel said they "probably" won't play (Yahoo Sports, Oct 20), against a Bears defense with 17 sacks, 5th in the league. Drake Maye gains more per throw (8.0 yards an attempt, 11 TD) but has thrown 7 interceptions; Caleb Williams is back from three missed games and completed 30 of 36 at Atlanta. Chicago runs for 160.7 yards a game, most in the NFL (ESPN team statistics, fetched Oct 21). Falsifier: if New England's patched line keeps Maye clean (zero sacks against the Jets on Sunday) and he does not turn it over, the Patriots win outright and the straight-up pick is wrong.

The Wednesday report was not posted when this was written (checked 8:30 AM PT Oct 21); for a Thursday game it is the one that carries game designations. The official reports so far are practice participation only (patriots.com, published Oct 21 9:12 AM, covering Monday and Tuesday). ESPN's game feed, updated Oct 21, already lists Alijah Vera-Tucker (G, hamstring) and Ben Brown (C/G, knee) as DOUBTFUL; both did not practice Monday or Tuesday, and Mike Vrabel said they "probably" won't play (Yahoo Sports via Heavy, Oct 20). Under the house rule doubtful means out. Morgan Moses (RT, foot) is listed QUESTIONABLE after leaving the Jets game; he was limited both days. Carlton Davis III (CB, neck) is questionable and was limited Tuesday. A.J. Brown (WR, ankle) was limited both days; he is not on ESPN's game feed. Romeo Doubs, New England's leading receiver by yards, is on injured reserve (ESPN roster).

If both guards sit, ESPN's depth chart puts Walter Rouse at left guard and Matt Pryor at right guard, with rookie Caleb Lomu behind Moses. Maye was not sacked Sunday (ESPN box score); that was with the starters for most of the game.

Chicago's side: RG Jonah Jackson (hip/quadriceps) QUESTIONABLE, limited Tuesday after not practicing Monday; Yahoo names Luke Newman or Jordan McFadden as the replacement. LB T.J. Edwards (hamstring), RB Kyle Monangai (toe/thumb) and LB Jack Sanborn (concussion) are questionable and did not practice either day. S Anthony Johnson Jr. went to IR. DE Montez Sweat serves the second game of a two-game suspension and DE Dayo Odeyingbo is on IR with a quadriceps injury (Yahoo, Oct 20), so Chicago's edge rush is Austin Booker plus whoever else steps up.

Where are Maye and Williams? their 2026 numbers against the 32 teams' leading passers, through Week 6

Answer: both are top-half passers, built differently. Maye is the downfield thrower: 7th of the 32 leading passers in yards per attempt, 7th in touchdowns, 6th in touchdown rate and 5th in quarterback rushing yards, but 30th in interceptions and interception rate. Williams is the efficient one: 1st in completion rate, 10th in passer rating, one interception, but his 9.0% sack rate is 27th (he took four Sunday) and he has played three games, so his counting stats rank low. Thursday puts the riskier thrower behind a patched line and the safer one behind a line that has allowed 15 sacks.

Missed tackles New England's number this season, and Chicago's

New England: 23 missed tackles, 25th of 32 (fewer is better); Chicago: 26, tied for 21st with Seattle (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, Oct 21). Denver has the most at 54, Detroit the fewest at 10.

Neither is a problem team. Both sit in the lower-middle of the league, three missed tackles apart.

Why it matters Thursday. Chicago's 160.7 rushing yards a game is the test for New England's tackling: Swift and Monangai have nine rushing touchdowns between them. New England's two lead backs average under four a carry, so the Bears' tacklers face less of that test.
