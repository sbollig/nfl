Tuesday practice reports are in; final reports and game statuses come Wednesday. For Kansas City, RB Kenneth Walker III (groin) was limited Tuesday after five straight reports as a non-participant and two missed games. Andy Reid said beforehand that Walker was "feeling pretty good" and that Tuesday's work would be important to the decision (ESPN player note citing Nate Taylor, Nov 24); CBS Sports (Nov 24) called it a "wait-and-see approach." ESPN's feed lists him QUESTIONABLE. RT Jaylon Moore (ankle) was the lone non-participant and has not practiced since his Week 9 injury (CBS Sports, Nov 24); ESPN lists him QUESTIONABLE. RG Trey Smith (concussion) had a second straight full practice but is still in the protocol; Reid said he anticipates Smith will be available (ESPN note citing Sam McDowell of The Kansas City Star, Nov 24). DT Omarr Norman-Lott (knee) was limited Tuesday and is close to a season debut after coming off the PUP list last week (CBS Sports). TE Noah Gray (ribs) was full Tuesday. FB Ben VanSumeren (knee) went on IR Tuesday.

Buffalo's side: five players were limited Tuesday (CBS Sports, Nov 24), all listed QUESTIONABLE on ESPN's feed: CB Christian Benford (groin), TE Dalton Kincaid (thumb/ankle), DT T.J. Sanders (shoulder), RG O'Cyrus Torrence (ankle) and LB Dorian Williams (calf). Benford, Kincaid, Sanders and Williams all missed Sunday's win over Miami. Kincaid told Matt Parrino of The Syracuse Post-Standard he will "most likely" play (ESPN note, Nov 24). Torrence has not missed a game since he was drafted in 2023 (CBS Sports). Greg Rousseau (back) was a full participant Tuesday after being limited Monday, so the league's No. 2 sack man (10.0) is expected to play (ESPN note, Nov 24).

What it means. Nobody on either side is doubtful or out yet, so this page plans with all of them and flags each one. If Walker sits, rookie Emmett Johnson starts a third straight game; he ran for 101 and 105 yards in the last two. If Moore sits, ESPN's depth chart has Joshua Ezeudu next at right tackle, opposite Rousseau.

Chiefs

Bills

Missed tackles Buffalo's number, Kansas City's, and the back who exploits it

Buffalo: 55 missed tackles, tied with Tampa Bay for fifth-most in the NFL; Kansas City: 39, tied for 27th (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, dated Nov 18, so before Week 11). Cleveland has the most at 79 and Detroit the fewest at 25.

Why it matters Thursday. Kansas City's backs are the ones who punish it. Emmett Johnson forced eight missed tackles against Arizona, his fourth game this season with more than five, and forced one on 41.1% of his touches (Next Gen Stats via NFL.com, Nov 22). Johnson also has 345 receiving yards with a long of 73, and Kenneth Walker III has a 76-yard run. Kansas City's 1,724 yards after the catch are nearly 400 more than Buffalo's 1,337 (ESPN team statistics).

The other side. The Chiefs have not allowed a run longer than 24 yards all season. James Cook III has a 74-yarder, from two weeks ago against the Jets. A Kansas City defense that tackles this well against the league's most productive rusher in this game is the quiet matchup inside the loud one.

Reassessment Mack's pick against the market, and what would move it

Where Mack and the market stand (Tuesday, frozen at the board build).

  • Line: Mack Bills −4.1 · market Bills −3 (opened −2.5)
  • Bills win probability: Mack 62% · ESPN Matchup Predictor 53.1% · moneyline Bills −149, Chiefs +126
  • Straight-up pick: Bills
  • Spread side: Bills −3
  • Total: Over 47.5 (opened 49.5)

How they compare. Same side, 1.1 points apart. The market has moved half a point toward Buffalo since the open and onto the key number of 3, with the Bills −3 priced at even money and the Chiefs +3 at −120, so the book is shading toward Kansas City at that number. ESPN's predictor is much closer to a coin flip than I am.

What would change it. Toward Buffalo: Walker ruled out, Smith not cleared from the protocol, or Moore out at right tackle against Rousseau. Toward Kansas City: Kincaid, Benford, Torrence or Dorian Williams ruled out on Wednesday; all four are in Buffalo's starting lineup on ESPN's depth chart. No quarterback is on either report.

Mack's view (judgment, not the formula). The Over is the pick I trust least. These offenses average 31.3 and 27.1 points, but the total has dropped two points since the open, Buffalo has not allowed more than 12 since its bye, Kansas City has allowed 13 or fewer three weeks running, and ESPN's game page shows 36 degrees, gusts to 27 mph and a 74% chance of precipitation. The side I like better than the total: Buffalo at home with the better kicker in bad weather.
