Where is Caleb Williams? his 2026 numbers against the other 31 teams' leading passers, through Week 11 — and Goff's alongside

Answer: top ten in accuracy and yards per attempt, bottom ten at protecting the ball. Against the other 31 teams' leading passers, Williams ranks 4th in completion rate (68.3%) and 8th in yards per attempt (7.9), and 9th in quarterback rushing yards despite playing seven games. The drag is interceptions: 6 in 218 attempts, a 2.8% rate that ranks 25th, with four of them in one game against Tampa Bay in Week 9. He missed Weeks 3–5 with a hamstring strain, which is why his counting totals sit below the median. After Sunday's win he said Chicago's offense can play much better (AP, Nov 22). Goff, for comparison, is 5th in rating (107.7), 5th in yards per attempt (8.2), 4th in yards per game (271.6) and 6th-lowest in interception rate (1.2%).

Missed tackles both teams rank in the league's top five at not missing

Detroit: 3.0 missed tackles a game, the fewest in the NFL; Chicago: 4.2 a game, fifth-fewest (nfllines.com team table, labeled through Week 12 of 2026, ten games each). The league average on that table is 5.1 and the worst mark is 7.9.

Two sound tackling teams, so the yards after contact have to be earned. That matters most against Gibbs, who has 1,098 rushing yards and another 404 receiving, and against a Bears defense that has allowed 4.5 yards a carry anyway. For Detroit the question is who is doing the tackling: Jack Campbell had 57 tackles in seven games before going on IR, and Barnes (49), Rodriguez (38) and Nowaske (30) have carried the second level since.

Chicago's leaders are a safety and a linebacker: Thieneman 64 tackles, Bush 61. Hicks, out with a concussion, had 12 in seven games.

Reassessment Mack has the Lions by 1.6; the market has the Bears by 2.5

Where the board stands (Tuesday). Mack's ratings make Detroit a 1.6-point favorite at home with a 55% win probability; the board market has Chicago −2.5. That is a 4.1-point disagreement, the kind that is usually about one thing, and here the one thing is Hutchinson. ESPN's Matchup Predictor agrees with the ratings on the winner: Lions 55.3%.

What the market is saying. The number opened Lions −2.5 and has crossed zero to the Bears since; the total has climbed four points from 51.5. Over that stretch Detroit lost Hutchinson for the season, Wonnum stopped practicing and Chicago's defense put up 6 sacks with Simmons getting 3.

What would change the pick. Wonnum and Clark inactive on top of Hutchinson, Campbell and Gill-Howard would take most of what is left of Detroit's front-seven production off the field, and I would no longer want the Lions straight up; Reed missing a fourth game would do the same to the coverage. On the other side, Gordon inactive with Hicks already out strengthens the Lions pick.

Mack's view (judgment, not the formula). The Under is the harder sell with two offenses averaging 28.8 and 26.2 points indoors, but Detroit has allowed 15, 9, 10 and 21 in its last four, Chicago 13 on Sunday, and 55.5 is four points higher than where this total opened. The side I like more is the Lions getting points at home, where they are 6-0.
