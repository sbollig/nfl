The readWashington is the home team, and it is the healthier one at quarterback: Jayden Daniels threw for 300 yards against San Francisco two weeks ago while Philadelphia starts Tanner McKee for the third time with Jalen Hurts on injured reserve for the season. The Eagles' five straight losses have come with an offense scoring 18.1 points a game and a defense that just gave up 204 rushing yards to Dallas without a sack, a tackle for loss or a quarterback hit. Washington has its own problems: Terry McLaurin went on IR Wednesday, the line has allowed a sack on 6.5% of dropbacks, and the defense allows 28.7 points a game, the worst in the league through the first five weeks by NFL.com's count. Mack's ratings make it Commanders −6.0 and 67%, a half point past the market; the pick is Washington on the spread and the Over on 40.5, and the one thing that would change it is Saquon Barkley's back.

Where is Tanner McKee? his 2026 numbers, two starts, set against the 32 teams' leading passers through Week 7

Answer: last in the league in yards per attempt, careful with the ball, and two games is all we have. McKee is not the Eagles' leading passer (Hurts still is, with 933 yards), so the table slots him into the 32-man list of each team's leader and asks where he would rank. His 5.3 yards an attempt would be 33rd of 33, below Jacoby Brissett and Cam Ward at 5.7; his 78.4 rating would be 28th. His interception rate (1.2%) would be top ten, and he is sacked at roughly the league-median rate behind a line that has allowed 18. Monday: 23 of 35 for 211, one touchdown to Makai Lemon, one interception; "I think it was just good execution," he said of the offense (philadelphiaeagles.com, Oct 27). Daniels, for comparison, ranks 11th in rating (97.6) among the 32 leaders, 1st in interception rate (zero), and 24th in sack rate (7.9%).

Coaching staffs tenure, experience, and what the beat has said this season

Eagles

  • Nick Sirianni, head coach since Jan 24, 2021; Wikipedia's infobox listed 61–31 regular season and 6–4 in the playoffs when fetched Oct 28, with its 2026 line still at 2–4 before Monday's loss. Won Super Bowl LIX after the 2024 season. After Dallas: "It's never just one thing when you give up a bunch of yards like that" (philadelphiaeagles.com, Oct 27). The Inquirer's Oct 28 notebook calls his future with the team "in question" and gives the Eagles a 4% playoff chance by The Athletic's simulator.
  • Sean Mannion, offensive coordinator, hired Jan 29, 2026: the fifth offensive coordinator in five years, after Kevin Patullo was let go Jan 13. Nine NFL seasons as a quarterback, then Packers offensive assistant (2024) and quarterbacks coach (2025).
  • Vic Fangio, defensive coordinator since 2024; in May he said "I'm good for two years, at least" (NBC Sports Philadelphia). The league's oldest coordinator.
  • Michael Clay, special teams coordinator; Parks Frazier coaches the quarterbacks.

Commanders

  • Dan Quinn, head coach since Feb 3, 2024; 12–5 and an NFC Championship Game loss to these Eagles in 2024, 5–12 in 2025, 1–4–1 now. Career 61–63–1 regular season and 5–3 in the playoffs per Wikipedia's infobox (fetched Oct 28), including 43–42 and a Super Bowl LI loss with Atlanta. Monday, on the Eagles week: "I'd love to have all four quarters together" (commanders.com, Oct 26).
  • David Blough, offensive coordinator, promoted from assistant quarterbacks coach Jan 9, 2026 at age 30; a former NFL quarterback who started coaching in 2024. First-time play-caller.
  • Daronte Jones, defensive coordinator, hired Jan 27, 2026 from the Vikings, where he was defensive backs coach and pass game coordinator for three seasons. Also a first-time NFL play-caller; Quinn said he runs his own system. Steve Wilks was added during the season to help with early-week defensive game planning (commanders.com, Oct 26).
  • Larry Izzo, special teams coordinator; D.J. Williams coaches the quarterbacks.

Missed tackles Philadelphia's number since Week 1, and Washington's

Philadelphia: 49 missed tackles, third-most in the NFL; Washington: 30, in a three-way tie for 21st with Jacksonville and New England (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, dated Oct 28 at 8:07 AM, so it includes Monday night). Denver leads with 56, Cleveland has 51, and the Lions have the fewest at 12. On a per-game basis the Eagles miss 6.0 a game (25th) and the Commanders 5.0 (16th), against a league average of 5.09 (nfllines.com, 2026 advanced defense table, fetched Oct 28).

Monday was the problem in one line. Dallas ran 38 times for 204 yards, Javonte Williams 25 for 155 with a 34-yard run, and the Eagles' defense finished with zero sacks, zero tackles for loss and zero quarterback hits (ESPN box score 401873009). Zack Baun led the team with 9 tackles, only 2 of them solo. Sirianni: "It's never just one thing when you give up a bunch of yards like that."

Why it matters Sunday. Washington's run game is Daniels first (213 yards on 25 carries, 8.5 each) and a committee of White, Ekeler and McNichols second, and the committee ran 17 times for 53 yards at San Francisco. Daniels is the one ball carrier on either side who turns a missed tackle into 30 yards. On the other side, Barkley's 29-yard run Monday was the Eagles' longest play, and Washington's tackling has been league-average; if Barkley sits, Philadelphia's backs are not the kind who break tackles on their own (Pierce 2.6 a carry).

Reassessment Mack's line and pick against the market, and what would move them

Where the board stands. Mack's line is Commanders −6.0 with Washington at 67% straight up; the market (DraftKings via ESPN, frozen at the board build) is Commanders −5.5 and 40.5, and ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Washington 64.1%. The half-point gap is not an edge worth chasing on its own; the pick is Washington because the ratings already price in a backup quarterback, an offensive line missing two starters to retirement and trade, and a defense that just gave up 204 rushing yards. ESPN's odds panel also shows an earlier number that reads as Eagles −2.5 and 46.5, but the page does not label it as the open; Football Nation recorded a FanDuel opening snapshot of Washington −3.5 and 40.5 on Oct 26. Either way the move has been toward Washington since Hurts went on IR.

What would change it. Barkley is the swing. With him the Eagles have the one player on the field Washington's 31st-ranked run defense cannot handle, and the spread side goes to even; without him Philadelphia's offense is McKee throwing to Lemon and Goedert, and the number should be higher than 6. On Washington's side, Mitchell's injury status for the Eagles and Omenihu's return are the next two inputs; Mariota only matters if Daniels is hurt. Under the formula a non-QB out costs −0.5, doubtful −0.3, questionable −0.15, capped at −2.5 a side; the Eagles carry seven questionables into Wednesday, Washington three.

Mack's view (judgment, not the formula). The Over at 40.5 is the stronger of the two calls. Washington's games have averaged 50.7 total points (304 in six), its defense allows 28.7 a game and an 81.8% opponent red-zone touchdown rate (Football Nation, via NFL.com), and the Eagles' seven games have still averaged 43.1 even with this offense. The Eagles have 24, 24, 7, 20, 17, 14 and 21; the Commanders have allowed 24, 37, 31, 30, 27 and 23. Two bad defenses and one mobile quarterback get past 40 more often than not.
