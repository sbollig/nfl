The readGreen Bay is the pick because of the one thing it does well: pressure without blitzing. Bryce Young has been pressured on 44.3% of dropbacks against four or fewer rushers, the highest rate among qualified passers, and the Packers get pressure on 38.5% of dropbacks without sending a fifth man (panthers.com, Oct 28). Young took five sacks Sunday behind a line that just lost Taylor Moton for the year, and left guard Damien Lewis has not practiced this week. The case for Carolina is that Green Bay cannot run (59.9 yards a game, 2.9 a carry, last in the league), its kicker has missed seven kicks, and it is the most penalized team in football. Mack's line at −5.8 is more than two points past the market; both numbers lean Green Bay, and the Under is a judgment call on a Packers offense scoring 20.6 a game.

This is the matchup of the game. Young has been pressured on 44.3% of dropbacks against four or fewer rushers, the highest rate among qualified passers, and 15 of his 17 sacks came on those plays; Green Bay pressures 38.5% of dropbacks without blitzing, fourth-highest (panthers.com, Oct 28). Young is far better against the blitz (0.17 yards over expected per dropback, fifth in the league), so Gannon has no reason to send one. Freeling's three sacks allowed all came in September, but Lewis's knee puts a backup at left guard next to a rookie tackle on the other side.

Where is Bryce Young? his 2026 numbers against the other 31 teams' leading passers, through Week 7

Answer: middle of the pack as a passer, top ten in touchdowns and touchdown rate, 19th at staying clean, and Sunday was the worst game of his season. Against the other 31 teams' leading passers, Young ranks 10th in passing yards, tied 12th in yards per attempt (7.5) and 9th in touchdown rate, but 28th in completion percentage (59.2) and 19th in sack rate. His rating of 92.9 is exactly the league median. Sunday against Tampa Bay he was 14 of 27 for 163 yards with two interceptions and five sacks for a 51.9 rating before Canales pulled him in the fourth quarter. Canales, Monday, via the Charlotte Observer's Scott Fowler: "I know Bryce. I just talked to him. He's ready to rock." Jordan Love, for comparison, sits 19th in rating at 90.6, 25th in completion rate and 8th in sack rate.

Missed tackles Green Bay's number since Week 1, and Carolina's

Green Bay: 38 missed tackles, tied for 12th-most in the NFL; Carolina: 23, tied for 29th (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, published Oct 28, so through Week 7). The Broncos have the most at 56, the Lions the fewest at 12.

The number flatters Carolina. A defense that misses few tackles but allows 5.2 yards a carry and 153.2 rushing yards a game is being beaten before the tackle point, not after it. Tampa Bay's 258 rushing yards Sunday came on 49 carries with runs of 46 and 29, and Carolina's front is its problem, not its tackling.

Why it matters Thursday. Green Bay's backs are not breaking tackles on their own: 2.9 yards a carry as a team, with MarShawn Lloyd at 2.6 on 54 carries and Josh Jacobs at 3.3 in his one game. If Carolina's front holds up the way it did not against Tampa Bay, the Packers' offense is Love throwing 45 times into a secondary with two healthy corners. Green Bay's 38 misses are the more relevant figure against Hubbard, who averages 4.8.

Reassessment Mack's line against the market, and what would move it

Where Mack and the market differ. Mack's line is Packers −5.8, 2.3 points past the market's −3.5. The market has moved toward Carolina since the open of Packers −7, and the total has climbed from 45.5 to 47.5 on ESPN's page (48.5 at the board build). ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Green Bay 64.7%, a shade under Mack's 67%. Everyone agrees on the winner; the disagreement is the margin.

What would change it. Golden out plus Glover out would leave Green Bay with a thin receiving corps behind Watson and a guard-and-center combination that has never played together; that is the scenario where −3.5 looks right and −5.8 looks rich. On the other side, Lewis and Phillips both out would cost Carolina a starting guard in front of a quarterback already sacked 17 times and its best edge rusher. Under the formula a non-QB out costs −0.5, doubtful −0.3, questionable −0.15, capped at −2.5 a side; with four questionables each the two sides roughly cancel.

Mack's view (judgment, not the formula). The Under is the weakest of the three calls. Carolina scores 26.5 a game and Green Bay throws two-thirds of the time against a secondary with two healthy corners; the case for the Under is Green Bay's 20.6 a game, a kicker who has missed seven kicks and a Carolina offense that managed 13 points Sunday. I would not argue with anyone who passes on the total.
