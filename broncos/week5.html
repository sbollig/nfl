The readThe game sits on two matchups: Denver's pass rush against a Chargers line without its left tackle and possibly its right, and a Denver secondary that may be without both Pat Surtain and Riley Moss against Tre' Harris and Ladd McConkey. The board's Broncos pick was built Tuesday, before either injury designation.
⚠ Secondary alert

Denver may be without both starting corners. Pat Surtain II is DOUBTFUL (Payton, Wednesday: does not expect him to play). Riley Moss is QUESTIONABLE with a rib injury (imaging Monday). The next men up are Jahdae Barron (also the backup nickel) and Kris Abrams-Draine, with Ja'Quan McMillian in the slot.

The Chargers' two leading receivers, Tre' Harris (13 catches, 209 yards, 16.1 per catch) and Ladd McConkey (12 for 183, 15.3 per catch), are exactly the kind of deep-ball targets a backup pair struggles with. Denver's coverage edge in the unit table below assumes Surtain plays; without him, treat that row as even at best, and the board's Broncos pick (built Tuesday, before these designations) as softer than its 60%.

Coaching staffs tenure, experience, and what the beat has said this season

⚠ Play-caller change, announced today

Sean Payton will call the plays Sunday. After Thursday's practice he said, “We'll make a change offensively … I'll call the plays,” taking the job from offensive coordinator Davis Webb for the Chargers game. Through four games the offense sits at 18.5 points a game (27th), 81.3 rushing yards (29th) and 199 passing yards (20th). Source: gazette.com, Oct 8, 3:14 PM

Broncos

HC Sean Payton — 4th season in Denver (hired early 2023). Saints head coach 2006–21 with a Super Bowl XLIV title; Giants OC 2000–02; Cowboys assistant HC 2003–05. Career 186–110 regular season, 10–10 playoffs.

OC Davis Webb — promoted February 2026 after Joe Lombardi was let go; Broncos QBs coach 2023–24, pass-game coordinator 2025. Called plays Weeks 1–4; Payton takes over Sunday (above).

DC Vance Joseph — Broncos DC since 2023; previously Broncos head coach 2017–18 (11–21) and Cardinals DC 2019–22. Interviewed for six head-coaching jobs last winter and returned.

STC Darren Rizzi — also assistant head coach, second year in the role; Saints special-teams coordinator from 2019 under Payton.

Critique. Fact: the offense ranks 27th in scoring and 29th rushing through four games, which is the stated reason for the play-calling change. Opinion (Mark Kiszla, Denver Gazette, Oct 4): called the Webb experiment “a failure,” faulting the third-and-long call sheet and a field-goal decision on 4th-and-2 at San Francisco.

Sources: Payton record · Webb promotion, NFL.com · 2026 staff, Gazette Mar 12 · Joseph hire, SI · Kiszla column

Chargers

HC Jim Harbaugh — 3rd season (five-year deal signed Jan 2024). 49ers head coach 2011–14, Michigan 2015–23, Stanford 2007–10. NFL career 66–35–1, 5–5 playoffs; playoff trips in both Chargers seasons. The 0–4 start is the franchise's first since 2017.

OC Mike McDaniel — first season, hired Jan 26, 2026 after Greg Roman was dismissed; Dolphins head coach 2022–25 (35–33, 0–2 playoffs), earlier 49ers OC.

DC Chris O'Leary — first season, age 34, hired Jan 30, 2026 to replace Jesse Minter (now Ravens head coach); Chargers safeties coach 2024, Western Michigan DC 2025.

STC Ryan Ficken — 5th season.

Critique. Fact: at Seattle, Herbert was sacked four times and hit nine before the two-minute warning, and the team drew 16 penalties; Harbaugh has declined to change coordinators (“we're going to keep growing”) and on Monday called a reporter's question about protection “more like an insult than a question.” Opinion (Roundtable, Oct 6): Ficken's special teams unit is described as on the hot seat. Two first-year coordinators and a rebuilt line are the structural story of the 0–4.

Missed tackles the number Denver's defense has carried since Week 1

Broncos defense — most missed tackles in the NFL

  • Season: 44 missed tackles through Week 4, most in the league (Eagles next at 36), per Pro Football Reference; PFF counts 48. Rush defense 142.8 yards a game, 30th.
  • Week 1 at Kansas City: 28 missed tackles, a 29.8% miss rate, 247 yards gained after missed tackles (Next Gen Stats); the third-worst single game since 2018. PFF counted 24.
  • Week 2 vs Jacksonville: 10 missed tackles. Weeks 3 and 4 per-game counts have not been published; in Week 4 Hufanga alone missed four (PFF).
  • Who is missing them (PFF, through Week 4): Talanoa Hufanga 8 · Riley Moss 6 · Ja'Quan McMillian 6 · Alex Singleton 6. Through Week 2, Moss's miss rate was 40%.

Why it matters Sunday. Three of the four leaders are the secondary (Hufanga, Moss, McMillian), and Moss is questionable. The Chargers' Omarion Hampton has forced 15 missed tackles on 59 carries with 3.12 yards after contact per attempt (PFF). A tackling-poor defense against a contact-heavy back is the Chargers' path to controlling the game.

Sources: PFR totals via BetMGM, Oct 7 · PFF counts, Yahoo Oct 6 · Week 1 NGS, Denver7 · Week 4 PFF grades · Hampton, PFF

Chargers defense

  • Season: 24 missed tackles through Week 4, 10th-most (Pro Football Reference). Per-game counts and a Next Gen Stats miss rate have not been published.
  • Week 4 at Seattle: three sacks; Tuipulotu had an interception and a forced fumble.

Denver's counter. J.K. Dobbins and RJ Harvey are averaging 3.6 a carry behind a line that has not produced a 20-yard run. Denver is more likely to beat missed tackles with Harvey's 20 catches out of the backfield than with the ground game.
