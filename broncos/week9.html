The readDenver arrives in first place in the AFC West after beating Kansas City 28-24 on a fourth-and-goal sack, with a 23-sack defense and a quarterback who has thrown three interceptions in eight games (ESPN, Nov 4). Carolina arrives off a 10-day break having fired its defensive coordinator (Ejiro Evero, Oct 30) after allowing 43 and 27 points in consecutive losses, and with Bryce Young sacked 11 times in those two games behind a line missing both starting tackles for the season (panthers.com, Nov 2–3). For the props: Denver's defense averages 2.9 sacks a game and Carolina's line has allowed 3.3; Carolina's defense has 11 sacks in seven games against a Denver line that has allowed 10 in eight. Takeaways run about 2.4 a game combined on season rates, and Carolina's games have averaged 53 points to Denver's 42. Mack's rating line is Broncos −6.4 against a market of −2.5 and he has the Over at 43.5; ESPN's predictor gives Denver 62.7%. Denver leads the series 6-2 in the regular season plus Super Bowl 50, and won the last meeting 28-14 in Denver in 2024 (panthers.com Know Your Foe, Nov 3). Denver's open questions are three defensive backs (Hufanga, Brandon Jones, Abrams-Draine); Carolina's are its left guard, edge rusher Jaelan Phillips, corner Will Lee III and whether Young's knee is what Dave Canales said it "maybe" was on Thursday night.

Coaching staffs tenure, experience, and what the beat has said this season

Broncos

  • Sean Payton, head coach since 2023. Sunday was his 200th career win including playoffs, the 12th coach to get there and the sixth-fastest, in his 300th regular-season game (denverbroncos.com, Nov 1); Wikipedia (fetched Nov 4) has him 37–21 in Denver with a 1–2 playoff mark and 190–110, 10–10 for his career. "There's nothing like it… I hate losing probably more than I like winning." He has called the plays since Week 5.
  • Davis Webb, offensive coordinator; Zach Strief, run game coordinator and assistant head coach; John Morton, pass game coordinator; Logan Kilgore, quarterbacks.
  • Vance Joseph, defensive coordinator since 2023; Robert Livingston, defensive pass game coordinator.
  • Darren Rizzi, special teams coordinator and assistant head coach.

Panthers

  • Dave Canales, third-year head coach, hired Jan 25, 2024 on a six-year deal after one season as Tampa Bay's offensive coordinator and 13 in Seattle. 5–12 in 2024, 8–9 and an NFC South title in 2025 (lost the wild-card game to the Rams 34–31), 3–4 now. On firing his coordinator: "a personal thing that I don't take lightly" (panthers.com, Nov 2).
  • Brad Idzik, offensive coordinator since 2024 (age 35; Buccaneers receivers coach 2023, Seahawks assistant 2019–22). Will Harriger coaches the quarterbacks; Harold Goodwin is run game coordinator.
  • Todd Wash, interim defensive coordinator and play-caller since Oct 30, when Ejiro Evero (the Broncos' coordinator in 2022) and passing game coordinator Jonathan Cooley were fired after the Green Bay loss. Wash, 58, was Jacksonville's coordinator 2016–20 (the 2017 AFC title game), Detroit's line coach 2021–22 and Carolina's line coach since Feb 2023. DeAngelo Hall was hired Nov 2 to coach the corners; Peter Hansen is assistant coordinator.
  • Tracy Smith, special teams coordinator.

Missed tackles Denver's number since Week 1, and Carolina's

Denver: 70 missed tackles, the most in the NFL; Carolina: 29, 29th of 32 (Pro Football Reference, via BetMGM, published Nov 4, so through Week 8). The Lions have the fewest at 15. Denver's count was 56 through Week 7, so Kansas City cost 14 missed tackles, the worst game of a bad year; Cleveland is second at 62 and nobody else is above 53.

Why it matters Sunday. Carolina's offense is Hubbard (4.6 a carry, 22 catches) and a quarterback who extends plays less than he did, by his own coach's account. Denver survived Sunday because Surtain and Skinner made goal-line stops on Walker; it allows 4.5 a carry on the year. Hubbard ran 22 times for 81 against Green Bay with a long of 18, so he is not breaking long runs on his own, but 70 missed tackles says Denver gives them away.

The other direction. Carolina misses fewer than half as many but still allows 4.8 a carry and 140.7 rushing yards a game, 30th in the league (panthers.com, Nov 3). That is a fit problem, not a tackling problem, and it is why the run game row below goes to Denver.

Reassessment Mack's line vs. the market, and the three props

Where Mack and the market stand (Wednesday). Mack's rating line is Broncos −6.4 and his straight-up pick is Denver at 68%; the market, frozen at the board build, is Broncos −2.5 and a total of 43.5. That is a 3.9-point disagreement in the same direction: both sides have Denver as the better team, Mack by nearly a touchdown on the ratings even after Carolina's home field. ESPN's live number Nov 4 was unchanged at −2.5 / 43.5 off an open of −3 / 42.5, and ESPN's Matchup Predictor has Denver at 62.7%.

The props. This week the board asks for Over/Under on total sacks, total takeaways and total points. The inputs: Denver's defense averages 2.9 sacks a game and Carolina's line has allowed 3.3 (five and six in its last two games); Carolina's defense averages 1.6 and Denver's line allows 1.3, with Carolina getting none at Green Bay. Takeaways: Denver has 10 (7 interceptions, 3 fumble recoveries) and has given the ball away 6 times in eight games; Carolina has 10 (4 interceptions, 6 fumbles) and has given it away 10 times in seven. Points: Denver scores 21.5 and allows 20.6; Carolina scores 25.4 and allows 27.6, and Carolina's games have averaged 53 points to Denver's 42. Mack's judgment, not the formula: sacks Over, because a 23-sack defense meets a line that has allowed 11 in two games with its left guard spot three-deep in questionables; takeaways Under, because Nix is 6th-lowest in interception rate, Denver has lost three fumbles all year, and Carolina's defense has four interceptions in seven games; points Over with the house pick, because Carolina's defense allows 27.6 and its own passing game is 4th in yards.

What would change it. Jones and Hufanga both out costs Denver −1.0 under the formula and leaves the deep secondary to Key, Rapp and Skinner against a quarterback who is fourth in passing yards; Mack's line would still be Denver by about five. Phillips and Lee back for Carolina adds its best pass rusher and best corner and takes the sacks lean back toward the number. Lewis cleared at left guard matters more than anyone on the Carolina list for the sacks prop. A Young knee setback would turn this into Pickett against Denver's front and move everything Denver's way.
